Understand the idea

1. What makes current flow?

A battery creates a potential difference between its terminals. In a closed conducting path, this electric field makes charges already present in the wire drift. The battery supplies energy to the circuit.

2. What does resistance do?

Collisions and interactions inside a material oppose the drift of charge. For an ohmic conductor at constant physical conditions, more voltage produces proportionally more current; more resistance at the same voltage means less current.

3. Connect the observation to the rule

Ohm's law describes the relationship: V = IR. To find current, rearrange it to I = V/R. For example, 6 V across 3 Ω gives I = 6/3 = 2 A.

V = IR
V

Potential difference, in volts (V).

I

Current, in amperes (A).

R

Resistance, in ohms (Ω).

Rearranged

I = V/R

Key idea

If resistance stays constant, doubling voltage doubles current. If voltage stays constant, doubling resistance halves current.

Now practise yourself

A 12 V battery is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. Before using the simulator, calculate the current and say why you divided instead of multiplied.

Answer check: I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A. We divide because the formula for current is voltage divided by resistance.
