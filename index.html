03 · Understand the rule

V = I × R
1
Voltage (V)
The electrical push across a component, measured in volts (V).
2
Current (I)
The rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes (A).
3
Resistance (R)
How strongly a component opposes current, measured in ohms (Ω).
For a fixed resistance: increasing voltage increases current in the same proportion.
For a fixed voltage: increasing resistance decreases current.

04 · Exam notes

  • Ohm’s law: for a conductor under constant physical conditions, current is directly proportional to potential difference across it.
  • Equation: V ∝ I, so V = IR. The constant R is resistance.
  • Rearrange: I = V/R and R = V/I.
  • Graph: for an ohmic conductor, the V–I graph is a straight line through the origin when V is plotted against I. Its slope is R.
  • Conditions: temperature and other physical conditions must remain constant.

05 · Worked example

Question: A resistor of 4 Ω is connected across 12 V. Find the current.

I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A

Step 1: Write the known values. Step 2: Choose I = V/R. Step 3: Substitute with units. Step 4: Give the answer in amperes.

Common mistake: multiplying V and R when current is required. For current, divide voltage by resistance.
